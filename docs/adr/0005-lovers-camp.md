# A mixed couple of Lovers forms a third Camp, derived from their current Roles

Cupid can make a Werewolf and a non-Werewolf Lovers. We follow the classic rule: such a couple forms the Lovers Camp and wins only if they are the last two Players alive, rather than simply keeping their original Camps with a shared death. Playing against your own Camp for love is the main reason Cupid is fun. A Player's Camp is therefore no longer a fixed property of their Role. It is derived from their current Role and their Lovers bond, and it is worked out again whenever either one changes (a Thief can rob a Lover, making a mixed couple same-sided, or the reverse).

While a mixed couple is alive, victory is checked like this:

- **Lovers** win when they are the only two Players alive.
- **Village** wins when no Werewolf at all is alive, the Werewolf Lover included (killing them kills their partner through grief).
- **Werewolves** win when the Werewolves outside the couple are at least as many as everyone else, both Lovers counted in "everyone else".

With no mixed couple alive, victory works as before. If a chain of Death triggers leaves nobody alive, nobody wins and the Game ends in a draw.

## Considered options

- **Lovers keep their original Camps and only share their death:** rejected. It is simpler, but it turns Cupid into a weaker, accidental Hunter and removes the betrayal play.
- **Counting the Werewolf Lover as a Werewolf in the majority check:** rejected. With one ordinary wolf plus a mixed couple, the Werewolves would win at once, although the couple can still vote that wolf out and win.
